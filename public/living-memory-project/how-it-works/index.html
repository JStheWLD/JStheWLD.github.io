Meet Maybelle.

She is an oral history AI. She gets the people who were there talking, keeps what they say, checks it, and carries it into the Living Memory.

She is the gold thread through every part of it. Each step below has a live demo.

WHO SHE IS

She asks like a reporter, and listens like a neighbor.

She knows the music well enough to relate, and the history well enough to ask the next good question. She is a team of AI agents, and she says she is an AI.

People can talk or type, as they like, and switch any time. As they talk, the names they say light up.

STATUS · LIVE, BY INVITATION

ONE ON ONE

Alone with her, she is your guide to the Living Memory.

Ask her about any name, and she shows you what is there and where it leads.

When you know something that is missing, she asks what you remember and keeps it, and it joins the Living Memory once a person approves it.

STATUS · PARTIAL · ONE-ON-ONE CONVERSATIONS ARE LIVE, BY INVITATION · THIS CONVERSATION IS AN EXAMPLE

LIVE DEMO · EXPLORING HAROLD BRADLEY

WITH TWO OR MORE PEOPLE

With people who were there, she runs a loop.

People aren’t comfortable being interviewed by an AI. In pairs and small groups they are wildly chatty, so mostly she listens.

Five steps: capture, verify, decide, invite, connect. Then it starts over.

1 OF 5 · CAPTURE

She listens, and maps it as people talk.

On a call with two or more people, the names she already knows light up as they are said. New names are added right after the call.

An institution’s own oral history interviews come in the same way, with its permission.

STATUS · TWO-PERSON CALLS: BUILT AND TESTED · GROUPS OF 3 TO 5: PLANNED · ARCHIVE INTERVIEWS: BUILT, ONE READ SO FAR

LIVE DEMO · RAY KENNEDY AND JOE GLASER

2 OF 5 · VERIFY

The only real proof is how many people agree.

Every line is checked against published records and against every other account in the Living Memory.

Several accounts agree Harold Bradley played the tic-tac bass on “I Fall to Pieces,” so that line is marked checked. One disagrees on the year, make and model of the bass, so that part is marked in question, and both versions are kept, each with the name of who told it.

STATUS · PARTIAL · RECORDS ARE SCORED BY HOW MANY INDEPENDENT SOURCES AGREE · DISAGREEMENTS KEEP BOTH SIDES · ACCOUNT-AGAINST-ACCOUNT CHECKING IS NOT AUTOMATIC YET

LIVE DEMO · CHECKING THE STORIES

2 OF 5 · VERIFY

Every line keeps its receipt.

Nothing appears under a person’s name until a person approves it.

Then the line carries where it came from: who said it, how it was recorded, what backs it, and who approved it. That record can’t be changed afterwards, so a museum, a label or a family can trust the line years from now.

STATUS · APPROVAL: BUILT, BY ONE PERSON TODAY · THE UNCHANGEABLE RECORD: TO BE CONFIRMED BEFORE THIS PAGE GOES LIVE

3 OF 5 · DECIDE

She reads the whole memory and sees where it’s thin.

When a part of the story has few first-hand accounts, she works out who to find. The Nashville sound is thin, so she looks for people who would want to talk about it and lands on Joe Glaser and Andy Reiss.

She also sees moments coming. Buddy Guy turned 90 this year. 2027 is the 75th anniversary of the Les Paul guitar.

4 OF 5 · INVITE

She asks both people before she introduces them.

We record people two at a time, talking to each other, because that’s where the best stories come out. We call these conversations Pairs.

The disagreement found in Verify becomes the first question: which bass did Harold Bradley play?

STATUS · INVITATIONS: LIVE · THIS INVITATION IS A DEMO

LIVE DEMO · THE HAROLD BRADLEY INVITATION

5 OF 5 · CONNECT

She sits in on the call, and every new name is a lead.

Once both people say yes and pick a time, Maybelle joins the call. If she can’t find someone who comes up, she asks the people on the call to introduce them.

Afterwards she sends clips of the conversation to everyone on it to share, and the loop starts over at Capture.

STATUS · NEW NAMES BECOME LEADS: BUILT · EVERY TELLER IS CREDITED: BUILT · CLIPS TO SHARE: PLANNED

LIVE DEMO · ANDY REISS AND JOE GLASER

INTO THE LIVING MEMORY

One sentence becomes names and lines.

After the call, each story is broken into the people, songs, places and things it names, and a line between them that says what was passed on. Every new line joins everything already in the Living Memory.

STATUS · BUILT, RIGHT AFTER THE CALL · THIS DRAWING IS AN ILLUSTRATION OF THE STEP, NOT THE LIVE DATA

OUT · ROOMS

In a room, a visitor hears it from the person who was there.

The same approved lines feed every place people meet them, and update as new stories come in.

In a corner of a museum, a visitor picks a name and hears the story in the teller’s own voice.
