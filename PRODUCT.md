# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Readers and fans discovering Jonathan Sexton's writing and music, arriving from The Storm Door
(Substack), word of mouth, or search. They're browsing to get a feel for who he is before
committing to anything. Often unhurried, often mobile, reading-first.

## Product Purpose

The story-house / home base for JS the Wld, Jonathan Sexton's author-musician platform. It
introduces him, links out to The Storm Door (the Saturday letter, on Substack), previews the book
(The Sound of Something More), and houses interactive pieces (The Field). Success is layered, in
order: (1) the visitor gets a true feel for who he is and what this is, (2) they subscribe to The
Storm Door, (3) they come away aware of the book. Identity first, conversion second — the site
earns the subscribe, it doesn't chase it.

## Brand Personality

Appalachian literary, handmade, warm and unhurried. "How we used to do it, and how we might
again." Built in analog textures, not digital gloss: taped photographs, torn paper edges, warm
paper grain, ledger/notebook ruling, corduroy ribbing, hand-drawn oaks and a truth-bird. Bodoni
Moda (display) paired with Libre Baskerville (body). Two skins in tension: Daylight (warm
eggshell, reading) and Lamplit (charcoal, photographic, hero/threshold moments) — never blended
into one flat look.

## Anti-references

Generic AI-slop SaaS marketing: cream/gradient body backgrounds, identical card grids, tiny
uppercase tracked eyebrows over every section, gradient text, glassmorphism-as-decoration. Nothing
cold, corporate, or engineered-feeling — every surface should read as made by hand by one person,
not generated. (Standing project doctrine, carried from the broader Sound of Something More
project: never crypto/blockchain/decentralization-as-technology visual language, even
incidentally.)

## Design Principles

1. **Identity before conversion.** The visitor should feel who this is before being asked to do
   anything. The subscribe ask ("the porch, not the funnel") comes after, not instead of.
2. **Handmade over slick.** Every texture reinforces analog warmth over polished digital gloss —
   tape, torn paper, grain, ink, never a flat vector shortcut standing in for the real thing.
3. **Two skins, never blended.** Daylight carries reading; Lamplit carries hero and threshold
   moments. Mixing them into one flat tone loses the effect both are built for.
4. **One thing wearing different clothes.** The songs, essays, and book share a single thesis;
   every surface should feel like the same person talking, in a different room.
5. **Show, don't oversell.** Specific and personal copy, never marketing-buzzword cadence; the
   reader should feel talked to, not marketed at.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard WCAG AA. `prefers-reduced-motion` already respected in global.css (`scroll-behavior`
short-circuits to `auto`). No additional stated needs.
